Discover Lahore

Lahore is one of the oldest cities in South Asia and a centre of art, literature, and cuisine. Known as the City of Gardens, it preserves magnificent Mughal-era monuments within its walled old city and along the banks of the Ravi River. Visitors come from across the world to witness its mosques, forts, and lush gardens that reflect the grandeur of the Mughal Empire.

Must-Visit Places in Lahore

Badshahi Mosque

Built in 1673 by Emperor Aurangzeb, the Badshahi Mosque is one of the largest mosques in the world. Its red sandstone walls, marble domes, and vast courtyard can hold tens of thousands of worshippers. Standing opposite the Lahore Fort, it remains the most iconic symbol of the city and a UNESCO World Heritage site alongside the fort complex.

Lahore Fort

The Lahore Fort, also known as Shahi Qila, has been rebuilt and expanded by many rulers over nearly a thousand years. Inside its walls lie the Sheesh Mahal (Palace of Mirrors), the Naulakha Pavilion, and ornate audience halls decorated with intricate frescoes. The fort tells the story of Punjab's rulers from the Ghaznavids to the British Raj.

Shalimar Gardens

Commissioned by Emperor Shah Jahan in 1641, Shalimar Gardens is a UNESCO-listed Persian-style charbagh garden spread over three descending terraces. Marble pavilions, flowing water channels, and hundreds of fountains create a peaceful retreat from the bustle of the city. Evening visits are especially popular when the gardens are illuminated and families gather along the walkways.

Plan Your Visit

The best time to visit Lahore is between October and March when the weather is pleasant for walking tours of the old city. Allow at least two full days to explore the fort, mosque, and gardens, and save time for Food Street near the fort and the colourful Anarkali Bazaar for traditional crafts and street food.
